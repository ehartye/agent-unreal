# Stylized look example scenes (Unreal 5.8)

Run the launcher commands below from the plugin root. They wrap each complete probe in the [shared owned process tree](../engine_lock/README.md); retained scientific results describe their original runs.

[`examples/unreal/stylized/`](stylized_validate.mjs) backs the Drawn, ink and cel looks section of [Niagara](../../../skills/unreal-effects/references/unreal-niagara.md) and the Substrate Toon bullets of [Substrate](../../../skills/unreal-rendering/references/unreal-substrate.md). It builds one probe level from a throwaway project (a station per look, each with its own camera and a backdrop wall) and measures engine screenshots; no external art. Five groups of stations: `base` (the original look checks), and the ink-brush groups `outline`, `ribbon`, `mesh` and `cost`.

```text
pwsh -NoProfile -File examples/unreal/engine_lock/with-engine-lock.ps1 node examples/unreal/stylized/stylized_validate.mjs --engine "C:/Program Files/Epic Games/UE_5.8" [--only base,outline,ribbon,mesh,cost] [--keep] [--shots <folder>] [--evidence <folder>]
```

It makes a project in the OS temp folder with `r.Substrate=True`, the Blendable GBuffer, `r.CustomDepth=3` (custom depth with stencil) and, in `[ConsoleVariables]`, `fx.ForceCompileOnLoad=1` (a Niagara system edited through the toolsets is not recompiled when another process loads it), plus a bare project for the `base` group. It runs the scripts: `stylized_cvar.py` (what a bare project gets), `stylized_build.py` (commandlet: procedural 4x4 ink-blob flipbook PNG, Toon Profile, Toon and unlit materials, Niagara systems authored through the toolsets, the level), `stylized_render.py` (offscreen Play-In-Editor, one engine screenshot per station, a simulation-cache read of the particles, compile states, and the cost windows) and `stylized_persist.py` twice (write, then read in a new process). `stylized_ink.py` holds the station table the build and render scripts share (the node test compares it with `stylized-ink.mjs`). `--only` limits the run to the named groups; the default runs all five and takes about 6 minutes (`base` about 2.5 minutes). Exit code 0 when every check passes. `--shots` copies nine `base` screenshots out; `--evidence` copies the reports, logs, every `stylized_*` screenshot, the CSV profiler files and the GPU readings of the run before the throwaway project is deleted. The run is manual and needs a GPU; `node scripts/check.mjs` runs only the pure part: `png-tools.mjs` (a small PNG reader, luminance bands, colour counts), `stylized-measure.mjs`, `stylized-ink.mjs` (the ring check, the frame difference, the CSV and `nvidia-smi` readers, the cost gate), `stylized-validate-checks.mjs` and `stylized-validate.test.mjs`.

## Ink-brush groups: outline, ribbon, mesh, cost (16 checks)

The look under test is the drawn flipbook (frame 9 of the generated atlas) and an unlit stepped-light material: `saturate(normal . light)` quantized to 4 levels with `floor`, mixed between a shadow and a bright colour. Every outline is the same flat colour, measured on a swatch station in each run, and a method counts as working when a captured frame shows a ring of that colour around the shape (the pixel check below), never by eye.

| Group | Station (system) | What it shows |
|---|---|---|
| outline | `o_swatch` | a flat sprite in the outline colour, the colour the ring check matches |
| outline | `o_none` | the drawn sprite with no outline method: the control |
| outline | `o_ramp` | coverage ramp: the flipbook alpha dilated by the largest of 16 taps (radius 0.04 and 0.02 of the sprite), the ring between the dilated and the original coverage in the outline colour, in the sprite's own material |
| outline | `o_flange` | coverage flange: a second sprite renderer on the same particles draws the dilated coverage in the outline colour, pushed away from the camera by world position offset, so the body covers it except at the rim |
| outline | `o_stencil_sprite`, `o_stencil_mesh` | stencil: `render_custom_depth` and a stencil value of 1 on the Niagara component, and a post process material (before bloom) that colours a pixel where the stencil is 0 and one of 16 pixels around it (radius 6 and 3) is above 0. Each is shot with the post process on and off |
| ribbon | `r_smooth`, `r_ink`, `r_edge`, `r_stencil` | a StaticBeam ribbon (local space, 2,400 long so its ends leave the frame, 250 wide, 100 particles); the material lights a cylinder normal across the ribbon width, in steps or smooth; `r_edge` colours the two long edges (coverage across the width above 0.8); `r_stencil` is `r_ink` with the stencil outline |
| mesh | `m_smooth`, `m_ink`, `m_stencil` | a mesh renderer (the engine sphere, scale 3) on a one-particle sprite emitter whose sprite renderer is switched off, with an override material lit from the world normal; `m_stencil` adds the stencil outline |
| cost | `c_control`, `c_ink`, `c_drawn` | 2,048 translucent sprites of 800 units stacked in one spot: a flat unlit control, the ink look (sphere normal, steps, a ring from the radial coverage) and the drawn look with the 16-tap outline |

Judging code (all pure, in `stylized-ink.mjs`, tested in `stylized-validate.test.mjs`):

- Ring: the shape is every pixel that differs from the backdrop by more than 14 per channel; its boundary is the shape pixels with a background pixel beside them (the frame edge counts as shape). A ring is found when at least 85% of the boundary pixels have an outline-coloured pixel (within 60 per channel of the swatch colour) within 4 pixels, there are at least 150 outline pixels, and at least 90% of them lie within 30 pixels of the boundary (a filled blob of the colour fails this). A stencil method also needs at least 300 pixels that differ between its stencil-on and stencil-off frames.
- Stepped: at least two flat bands, at most 8 luminance levels and a flat share of at least 0.5, against a smooth control with at least 12 levels and a flat share of at most 0.5 (a gradient clamps flat at its ends).
- Cost gate: the CSV profiler records 300 frames per window (`CsvProfile STARTFILE=<name>`, then `FRAMES=300`), each scene in 2 interleaved rounds; the figure is the median `GPUTime` after the first 10 frames. The ink-minus-control difference is kept only when the GPU use read by `nvidia-smi` (`utilization.gpu`) before the run (5 readings) and after it (5 readings), when this probe's process is not running, is under 10 percent, and the two rounds of each scene agree within 10 percent. Otherwise the answer is "not measured" with the readings. Readings during the run include the editor itself and are kept as a record. Without `nvidia-smi` the answer is "not measured" too.
- The pass or fail of a method check follows a table (`INK_EXPECTED` in `stylized-validate-checks.mjs`), so a method that does not work is a recorded negative result and not a failing probe; change a value only when a run shows the other result.

Status (three runs on a GPU machine, each covering all four groups; the runs are one invocation each, evidence kept with the run log):

- outline: validated for the stencil outline and the coverage ramp, not for the flange as built. The stencil outline drew a ring on the sprite renderer (5,492 outline pixels, ring coverage 1.0, all of them differing from the stencil-off frame) and on the mesh renderer (5,104); it needed `r.CustomDepth=3`, the stencil value on the Niagara component and the post process. The coverage ramp drew a ring (16,734 pixels, coverage 1.0, hugging 1.0) once the taps were plain texture samples at a fixed cell of the atlas: a SubUV sample node gave no ring (run 1), and with a sub-image size of 4 by 4 on the renderer the sprite UV did not span 0 to 1 and the samples read a magnified part of the cell (run 2). The flange compiled once its offset stopped using `CameraVectorWS`, which is not available in the vertex shader (run 1), but it drew a displaced, enlarged copy and not a ring (coverage 0.70, 19,367 pixels, hugging 0.91); the push was probably not along the view ray (an inference from the frame). Smallest next experiment: push the flange along the camera's forward vector, with a `Transform` node taking (0, 0, 25) from camera space to world, and compare the frame. `MaterialProperty` has no pixel depth offset member, so the depth push is a world position offset. Overlap of several particles (whether the body of one hides the flange of its neighbour) was not tested.
- ribbon: validated. The ink material on a ribbon renderer drew 4 flat bands (flat share 0.96) against a 13-level gradient, the edge ramp drew a ring on both long edges (58,880 pixels, coverage 1.0), and the stencil outline drew a ring (15,360 pixels). The same results in all three runs. UV0 `V` runs across the ribbon width with the default `ScaledUniformly` mode.
- mesh: validated. The ink material on a mesh-particle renderer drew 4 flat bands (flat share 0.97) against a 17-level gradient, and the stencil outline drew a ring (5,104 pixels). The same in all three runs. The material is set per renderer (`bOverrideMaterials`, `explicitMat`).
- cost: measured. 2,048 stacked translucent sprites (viewport 1014 by 550, 290 frames per window after trimming, two rounds per scene agreeing within 0.2%): control 3.335 ms, ink look 3.339 ms, so the ink look cost +0.004 ms (run 2: -0.002 ms); the drawn look with the 16-tap outline cost 6.448 ms (+3.11 ms). The other GPU users read 1 to 2 percent before and after each run. With 256 sprites all three scenes read 1.33 ms (run 1), so the load is what raises the time. The scenes are blend-bound by construction, so the result says that the ink maths adds no measurable time when blending dominates, and that 16 extra texture reads per pixel add about 3 ms at 2,048 overlapping layers; it does not say what the look costs on a sparse effect. The ink and control scenes were not repeated on another machine.
